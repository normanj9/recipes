# Laab Salad Meal Prep

_Adapted from the Laab Moo recipe on hot-thai-kitchen.com, scaled up and reworked into a salad for weekly lunch prep._

## Ingredients

### Toasted Rice Powder
- 120g (2/3 cup) jasmine or Thai glutinous rice, raw
- 2 makrut lime leaves

### Laab
- 907g (2 lb) ground pork
- 105ml (7 tbsp) fish sauce, divided
- 90ml (6 tbsp) water
- 90g (about 3 medium) shallots, thinly julienned
- 6 makrut lime leaves, finely julienned
- 110ml (about 1/2 cup) lime juice (5–6 limes)
- 1.5–2.5 tbsp chili flakes, to taste
- 1/2–3/4 tsp MSG

### Edamame
- 340g (12 oz) shelled edamame, frozen
- 5g (1 tsp) salt

### Greens Dressing
- 45ml (3 tbsp) fish sauce
- 75ml (5 tbsp) lime juice
- 12g (1 tbsp) sugar
- 30ml (2 tbsp) water
- 1 tsp chili flakes

### Salad Base
- 280g (about 10 cups) baby spinach
- 450g (about 1/2 small head) green cabbage, finely shredded
- 1 large English cucumber

### Herbs (add day-of)
- 20g (1 loosely packed cup) mint leaves, larger leaves torn
- 1 bunch cilantro, roughly chopped
- 4 green onions, chopped

### Crunch (add day-of)
- Reserved toasted rice powder
- 40g (1/2 cup) fried shallots
- 70g (1/2 cup) roasted peanuts, crushed (optional)

---

## Method

### 1. Toast and Grind the Rice
In a large pot (the same one you'll cook the pork in) over medium-high heat, toast the raw rice and the 2 whole lime leaves, stirring constantly, until the rice is deeply browned. Pull the lime leaves as soon as they're dry and crispy, which will happen before the rice is done. Tip the rice out of the pot right away so it doesn't keep toasting. Once slightly cooled, grind the rice and leaves in a coffee grinder or mortar and pestle to a sandy texture (discard any leaf stems left in the mortar). About three-quarters of this goes into the laab; save the rest in a sealed jar for topping.

### 2. Cook the Edamame
Bring a pot of water to a boil with the salt. Add the frozen edamame and cook 3–4 minutes, until tender but still firm. Drain and let cool. Keep the salt light: the edamame gets tossed with the laab, and the fish sauce seasons them too.

### 3. Cook the Laab
Add about 35ml (2.5 tbsp) of the fish sauce to the pork and mash with a fork to mix it in and break up the ground-meat strands.

Bring the water to a boil in the pot over high heat. Add the pork and stir to break it up until just cooked through (there's a lot of meat, so this takes a few minutes longer than the original). Pull it off the heat right away; it gets reheated at lunch, so don't overcook it now.

While the pork is still hot, stir in the shallots and julienned lime leaves so the shallots wilt, separating the layers as you go. Then add the remaining fish sauce, the chili flakes, MSG, lime juice, and about three-quarters of the toasted rice powder. Mix well, then taste and adjust: fish sauce is the only salt here, so add more of it rather than salt. Fold in the edamame.

### 4. Make the Greens Dressing
Shake the fish sauce, lime juice, sugar, water, and chili flakes in a jar until the sugar dissolves. It's there to dress the spinach and cabbage so the plain greens don't dilute the laab, and it should taste a little sweeter and less punchy than the laab.

### 5. Prep the Base and Herbs
Toss the spinach and shredded cabbage together. Wash the herbs, dry them well, and store them together in a container lined with a paper towel. Mint blackens once it's cut and wet, so tear it day-of if you can. Leave the cucumber whole and slice it day-of.

### 6. Assemble Storage Containers
Divide the laab and edamame into 5 microwave-safe containers. Store the spinach and cabbage mix in its own container (or divide it into 5), separate from the meat, so the greens don't wilt.

### 7. Serve
Microwave a portion of laab for 60–90 seconds, just until warm, since the pork dries out quickly. Lime fades when it's heated, so add a squeeze of fresh lime if it tastes flat. Toss a portion of the greens with 1–2 tbsp of dressing and add sliced cucumber. Pile the warm laab on top, let it cool for a minute so the herbs don't wilt, then add the herbs, a sprinkle of the reserved rice powder, fried shallots, and peanuts.
