# Sous Vide Chile-Smoked Pulled Pork (Country-Style Ribs)

_A personal sous vide workflow._

## Ingredients

### Pork
- Boneless country-style pork ribs (Costco), cut into 2–3 inch chunks: ~900 g / 2 lb

### Dried Chile Powder (makes ~1-1½ Tbsp)
- Dried ancho chiles: 1 whole  
- Dried guajillo chiles: 1 whole  
- Dried árbol chile (optional, for heat): ½ whole

### Seasoning & Aromatics
- Kosher salt (Diamond Crystal): 10–12 g / ~2 tsp (1–1.25% by weight)  
- Frozen minced garlic: 6–8 g / 1–1½ tsp  
- Chile powder (from above): 4–6 g / 1–1½ tsp  
- Liquid smoke: 1–2 g / ¼–½ tsp  
- Neutral oil or rendered pork fat (optional): 10 g / 2 tsp

---

## Method

### 1. Make the Chile Powder
1. Remove stems from dried chiles and shake out loose seeds.  
2. Toast chiles in a dry skillet over medium heat, pressing gently, until pliable and fragrant (10–20 seconds per side). Do not scorch.  
3. Cool completely.  
4. Tear into pieces and grind to a fine powder in a spice grinder.  
5. Measure out 1–1½ tsp for the recipe; reserve extra for future use.

### 2. Season & Bag
1. Cut pork into 2–3 inch chunks for even cooking and increased surface area.  
2. Toss pork with salt, chile powder, garlic, liquid smoke, and optional oil until evenly coated.  
3. Vacuum seal in a single layer if possible.

### 3. Freeze (Optional)
1. Freeze immediately if not cooking right away.  
2. Pork can go straight from freezer to water bath when ready.

### 4. Sous Vide
1. Preheat water bath to **154°F / 68°C**.  
2. Cook pork for **26–28 hours**.  
3. Ensure bag remains fully submerged.

### 5. Pull & Separate
1. Remove pork from bag while hot.  
2. Pour bag liquid into a gravy separator.  
3. Pull pork into strands using forks or hands; keep warm.

### 6. Reduce & Recombine
1. Let fat separate fully, then remove and reserve it.  
2. Reduce the defatted liquid gently until lightly syrupy (about 50–65% reduction).  
3. Whisk in **¼–⅓ of the reserved fat** until glossy.  
4. Gradually toss sauce into pulled pork until moist and lightly coated.

### 7. Serve or Store
1. If serving immediately, use pork as-is for tacos, burritos, or rice bowls. Can crisp up portions in a foil-lined sheet pan under the broiler or on the stove in a skillet.  
2. For later use, transfer pulled pork and sauce to airtight containers.  
3. Let cool uncovered for 15–20 minutes, then seal and refrigerate.
